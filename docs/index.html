How this is measured

Fixed snapshots are selected at 12 hours before the reporting day, then 08:00, 15:00, and 20:00 reporting time. Each cutoff uses the newest snapshot from the preceding six hours.

Temperature bias and 80% interval coverage use actual reported settlement values only. An open-ended winning bracket is never converted into an invented temperature.

Candidate corrections use earlier dates for training and the most recent 20 settled dates for a holdout. They do not automatically change the model or enable paper orders.

Weather contracts, in forecaster language

Forecast the event, then judge the price

A contract is one precisely defined weather outcome, such as the daily high falling in 80–81°F at a named station. YES wins if that outcome happens; NO wins if it does not. For a temperature range, NO includes both colder and warmer outcomes.

In an ordinary binary settlement, a winning contract pays $1 and a losing one pays $0. A 40¢ purchase therefore risks 40¢ plus fees and can gain 60¢ minus fees if held to a winning settlement. The 40¢ price is roughly a 40% probability before fees, not an official weather forecast.

A likely outcome is not necessarily a good purchase. A 70% forecast offered at 85¢ costs more than its modeled chance of paying $1. Compare your probability to the fee-inclusive break-even percentage in the calculator.

Examples assume an ordinary $1/$0 outcome and holding to settlement. Selling early, special settlement rules, or a changed purchase price can produce different results.

Your meteorological advantage starts here

  • Use the exact station and reporting period. Regional PoP and an airport’s final daily precipitation total are different targets.
  • Ask what could move the high across a bracket boundary: cloud persistence, advection, frontal timing, or boundary-layer mixing.
  • For rain, examine station-scale coverage, storm motion, timing, and the remaining reporting window.
  • Record your probability and reasoning before the outcome. Forecast skill takes repeated verification to establish.

Those are forecast questions to investigate. The desk does not diagnose cloud, radar, or frontal trends from temperature curves alone.

A small trading vocabulary

Contract / bracket
The exact outcome, such as a two-degree temperature range.
Ask / purchase price
What a seller currently wants for one contract. It may change before you buy.
Bid / sell price
What a buyer currently offers if you want to sell. The gap between bid and ask is the spread.
Break-even probability
The chance of winning needed to cover the purchase price and estimated fees on average.
Edge / model advantage
Modeled expected payout minus purchase cost and fees. It depends on the probability being accurate.
Depth
How many contracts are available at a quoted price. More may cost a different price.
Settlement
Kalshi determines the outcome using the contract’s named data source and rules.
Paper practice
An example or recorded proposal with no real order and no assumed fill.

A limit order specifies your maximum purchase price; it may not fill. Always check the final price, fees, source, and reporting window on Kalshi.
